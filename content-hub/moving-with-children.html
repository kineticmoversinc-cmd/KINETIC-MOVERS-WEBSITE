Why Moving Affects Children

How Moving Affects Kids: What Parents Should Know

For children, a move isn't just a change of address — it can feel like losing their entire world. Understanding this perspective is the first step in helping them cope.

Common Emotional Responses

Children may show a move is weighing on them through:

  • Anxiety about the unknown and what comes next
  • Sadness over leaving friends and familiar places
  • Anger at having no control over the decision
  • Fear of not fitting in at a new school
  • Regression to earlier behaviours (clinginess, bedwetting)
  • Physical complaints like headaches or stomach aches

How Kids Understand a Move by Age

Tailor your moving with kids tips to their stage:

  • Toddlers (1-3 years): May not grasp the concept but sense parental stress
  • Preschoolers (3-5 years): May worry about losing toys or believe they caused the move
  • School-age (6-12 years): Understand more but worry about friends and fitting in
  • Teenagers (13-18 years): Often resist most strongly due to established social networks

Changing Schools After an Ontario Move

School transitions are often the biggest worry for kids — and parents:

  • Contact the new school or board as soon as your address is confirmed
  • Have proof of address and your child's immunization record ready
  • The Ontario Student Record (OSR) transfers between Ontario schools automatically
  • Ask about class placement, bus routes, and before/after-school programs early
  • Moving between public, Catholic, French, or private systems? Confirm which board serves your new address
Age-Appropriate Strategies

Moving with Children Checklist by Age Group

01

Toddlers & Preschoolers (1-5 years)

When it comes to how to move with toddlers, keep it simple and reassuring:

  • Use simple language: "We're moving to a new house where we'll all live together"
  • Read books about moving together
  • Let them help pack a special box of favourite toys
  • Keep naps, meals, and bedtime routines as consistent as possible
  • Visit the new home and neighbourhood before moving day
  • Let them express feelings through play or drawing
02

School-Age Children (6-12 years)

Older children benefit from involvement and information:

  • Explain the reasons for the move in age-appropriate terms
  • Show them maps of the new area and school
  • Involve them in house-hunting (if applicable)
  • Let them choose paint colours or décor for their new room
  • Research schools, parks, and activities in the new area together
  • Create a memory book of their current home and friends
  • Plan ways to stay in touch with current friends
03

Teenagers (13-18 years)

Teens need respect for their social connections and autonomy:

  • Acknowledge that leaving friends is a real loss
  • Involve them in decision-making when possible
  • Help them research clubs, sports, or activities in the new area
  • Plan a going-away party with current friends
  • Schedule visits back to see old friends after the move
  • Respect their need for privacy and space during the transition
  • Encourage them to document the move through photography or journaling
Practical Moving Day Tips

Moving Day with Kids: Routines & the Essentials Box

Before Moving Day

Preparation reduces anxiety for everyone:

  • Pack a kids' essentials box for each child: pyjamas, favourite stuffed animal, books, nightlight, and tomorrow's outfit
  • Arrange childcare on moving day if possible — movers, doors, and heavy furniture are not kid-friendly
  • If they'll be present, give them special jobs like checking off boxes
  • Explain what will happen step by step so there are no surprises
  • Take photos of their current room to recreate favourites in the new home

During the Move

Keep children safe, fed, and out of the movers' path:

  • Have snacks, drinks, and activities readily available
  • Create a "safe zone" in each home with familiar items
  • Keep regular meal and snack times even when the day runs long
  • Take breaks together to check in on how they're feeling
  • Celebrate small milestones (first box packed, truck loaded)
  • Keep the kids' essentials box in your car — never in the truck

After Moving In

Help them settle into the new environment:

  • Set up their room first to create a familiar sanctuary
  • Unpack the kids' essentials box before anything else
  • Explore the new neighbourhood together as an adventure
  • Find nearby parks, libraries, and community centres
  • Keep connections with old friends through video calls
  • Be patient — adjustment can take weeks or months
School Transition Tips

Changing Schools After an Ontario Move

Registering at the New School

Handle the paperwork before the first day:

  • Contact the school or board as soon as your address is confirmed
  • Bring proof of address and your child's immunization record
  • The Ontario Student Record (OSR) transfers automatically between Ontario schools
  • Ask about class placement, bus routes, and lunch programs
  • Visit the school together before the first day if possible

During the First Weeks

Support their social and academic adjustment:

  • Encourage participation in clubs, teams, or extracurricular activities
  • Facilitate playdates with classmates
  • Keep communication open with teachers about progress
  • Establish a steady morning routine before school starts
  • Pack a special note in their lunchbox for the first week
  • Celebrate small successes and efforts

Ongoing Support

Long-term adjustment continues:

  • Watch for signs of bullying or social isolation
  • Help them balance old friendships with new ones
  • Remind them it's okay to miss their old home and friends
  • Create new family traditions in your new home
  • Consider school counselling support if adjustment difficulties persist
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I transfer my child to a new school in Ontario?

Contact the new school or school board as soon as your address is confirmed. You'll typically need proof of address and your child's immunization record. The Ontario Student Record (OSR) transfers between Ontario schools automatically, so ask the new school if they need anything else for class placement.

How can I keep my toddler's routine during a move?

Keep meals, naps, and bedtime consistent, pack a comfort kit with a favourite blanket, snacks, and a nightlight, and set up their sleep space first at the new home. Familiar routines are the fastest way to help toddlers feel safe.

Should my kids be home on moving day?

If possible, arrange childcare with a friend or family member so kids stay clear of the movers' path and heavy furniture. If they must be there, set up a safe zone in one room with familiar items, snacks, and activities.

When should I tell my kids we're moving?

Tell them as early as you reasonably can — ideally 4 to 6 weeks out — using simple, positive language. Involve them in age-appropriate choices, like picking paint colours for their new room, to give them a sense of control.
