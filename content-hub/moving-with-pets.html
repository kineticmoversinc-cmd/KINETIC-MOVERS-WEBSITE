Moving with Pets Tips: A Guide for Ontario Pet Owners

Moving with pets takes extra planning — from vet records and health certificates to keeping dogs and cats safe on moving day. These moving with pets tips cover dogs, cats, and long drives along the 401 corridor, including pet-friendly stops and overnight options.

Why Moving Stresses Pets

Understanding Your Pet's Perspective on Moving

Pets thrive on routine and familiarity. A move disrupts their entire world — territory, scent markers, feeding schedules, and safe spaces. Understanding their stress points helps you plan accordingly.

Common Stress Triggers

Pets may experience anxiety due to:

  • Unfamiliar packing materials and boxes appearing everywhere
  • Strangers (movers) in their home and open doors
  • Disrupted feeding and walking schedules
  • Unfamiliar vehicle and motion during transit
  • New territory with unknown smells and sounds
  • Separation from family during loading and unloading

Signs of Moving-Related Stress

Watch for these indicators that your pet is struggling:

  • Changes in appetite (eating more or less)
  • Increased vocalization (whining, meowing, barking)
  • Destructive behaviour (chewing, scratching)
  • Hiding or withdrawal from family
  • Changes in litter box habits
  • Excessive grooming or licking
  • Aggression or unusual fearfulness

Ontario-Specific Considerations

Moving across Ontario presents unique factors for pets:

  • Municipal bylaws differ — Toronto requires dog licences, and leash and pet limits vary by city
  • Transitioning between urban condos and suburban homes changes noise and routine
  • Varying access to green spaces, dog parks, and off-leash areas
  • Seasonal weather swings: heat, humidity, cold snaps, and road salt
  • Longer drives may need planned comfort stops along the 401 corridor
Pet-Specific Plans

How to Move with a Dog & Moving with Cats Checklist

Dogs and cats experience a move very differently — plan for each species separately.

01

Vet Records & Health Certificates

Before moving, make sure your pet is healthy and documented for the transition:

  • Schedule a wellness check-up and update vaccinations
  • Request copies of medical records and vaccination history for your new vet
  • Ask for a health certificate if you're crossing provincial borders
  • Discuss calming or anti-anxiety options if your pet is a nervous traveller
  • Get recommendations for veterinarians in your new area
  • Update microchip details and tags with your new address and phone number
02

How to Move with a Dog

Dogs cope best with exercise, familiarity, and a clear transport plan:

  • Keep the walk and feeding schedule as normal as possible
  • Exercise your dog well the morning of moving day to burn off nervous energy
  • Transport in your own vehicle with a secured crate or harness — never in the moving truck
  • Pack a dog kit: leash, food, water, bowls, waste bags, and a favourite toy
  • Bring the crate or bedding out early so it smells like home
  • For long drives, plan comfort stops every 2–3 hours along your route
03

Moving with Cats Checklist

Cats are territorial — routine and confinement are your best tools:

  • Leave the carrier out for several days before the move so it isn't a surprise
  • Keep your cat in one quiet room during packing with litter, water, and familiar bedding
  • Maintain the exact feeding routine on moving day
  • Move the cat in your own car, in a secure carrier, last — after the truck is loaded
  • Pack a cat kit: litter and box, food, water, carrier, and a worn T-shirt that smells like you
  • Consider a vet-approved calming aid for anxious cats
Moving Day

Keeping Pets Safe & Clear of the Movers

Moving day is the riskiest part for pets — open doors, heavy furniture, and strangers. A little planning keeps everyone safe.

01

Set Up a Pet Safe Room

Designate one room as off-limits at each home:

  • Choose a room the movers won't need — a bathroom or spare bedroom works well
  • Set it up with water, food, bedding, toys, and a litter box for cats
  • Put a clear "PET INSIDE — DO NOT OPEN" sign on the door
  • Tell the crew lead about the room at the start of the day
  • Check on your pet regularly between loading and unloading
02

Transport Pets Yourself

Movers cannot transport pets — they ride with you:

  • Dogs travel in a secured crate or with a crash-tested harness
  • Cats travel in a hard-sided carrier, never loose in the car
  • Never leave pets in a parked car — temperatures turn dangerous in minutes
  • Bring water and offer it at every stop
  • Keep collars with ID tags on at all times during the move
  • Have recent photos of each pet in case one slips out a door
03

ID, Bylaws & Emergency Prep

Prevent lost-pet scenarios before they happen:

  • Secure collar with an ID tag that's readable and current
  • Add a temporary tag with your cell number for moving week
  • Know your pet's hiding spots in both homes
  • Look up animal shelters and emergency vets in your new area in advance
  • Check local bylaws — Toronto requires dog licences, and leash rules vary by municipality
Travel & Settling In

401-Corridor Pet Travel & Settling Into the New Home

Many Ontario moves mean hours on Highway 401. Plan the drive — and the first days in the new home — with your pet in mind.

Pet-Friendly Stops Along the 401

Plan comfort breaks before you need them:

  • ONroute service centres along the 401 have grassy areas suitable for leashed dog breaks
  • Schedule a stop every 2–3 hours for water, food, and a bathroom break
  • If your move needs an overnight stay, look for pet-friendly hotels in Ontario corridor cities like Kingston, Belleville, or Trenton — confirm the pet policy and any fees when booking
  • Pack a travel kit within arm's reach: leash, water, collapsible bowls, food, waste bags, and any medications

Settling Dogs Into the New Home

Familiarity first, exploration second:

  • Set up their bed, bowls, and toys in one room before letting them roam
  • Walk the new neighbourhood on-leash the first few days to build confidence
  • Keep feeding and walk times consistent with the old routine
  • Introduce other household pets gradually, on neutral ground where possible

Settling Cats Into the New Home

Start small and expand slowly:

  • Begin with one room: litter box, food, water, scratcher, and hiding spots
  • Open up the rest of the home gradually over several days
  • Rub a cloth on your cat's cheeks and dab it on doorways to spread familiar scent
  • Keep cats indoors for at least 2–4 weeks before any supervised outdoor access
  • Update microchip and licence details the week you arrive
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do moving companies transport pets?

No — movers cannot carry pets in the truck. Plan to move your dog or cat in your own vehicle in a secure crate or carrier, and keep them clear of the loading area on moving day so the crew can work safely.

What vet paperwork do I need when moving with pets?

Ask your vet for vaccination records, medical history, and a health certificate if you're crossing provincial borders. Update your microchip details with the new address and register with a new vet early.

Are there pet-friendly hotels along the 401 in Ontario?

Yes — many hotel chains in corridor cities like Kingston, Belleville, and Trenton offer pet-friendly rooms. Book in advance, confirm the pet policy and any fees, and plan regular comfort stops for your dog or cat along the way.

How do I keep my cat calm on moving day?

Set up a quiet safe room away from the movers with the carrier, litter, water, and familiar bedding. Keep the feeding routine normal, close doors and windows, and move the cat in your own car last so it spends the least time in transit.
