Beyond the Moving Truck

What a Move Actually Costs

The moving company's quote is usually the single biggest line item, but it's rarely the only one. Here's what else typically shows up on a moving budget.

The Move Itself

The core cost, driven by a few key factors.

  • Hourly or flat-rate crew and truck fees (varies by home size and distance)
  • Packing materials if you're not supplying your own boxes
  • Full-service packing/unpacking labour, if you choose to add it
  • Extra fees for stairs, long carries, or difficult building access

Before Moving Day

Costs that show up while you're still preparing.

  • Packing supplies: boxes, tape, bubble wrap, markers
  • Deep cleaning supplies (or a cleaning service) for your old home
  • Storage rental if there's a gap between move-out and move-in
  • Time off work to pack, coordinate, and supervise moving day

After You Arrive

Setup costs at the new address that are easy to forget.

  • Utility connection or transfer fees (hydro, gas, internet)
  • Address change costs (mail forwarding, new ID, licence plates)
  • New locks or a locksmith for a previous owner's keys
  • Immediate repairs or touch-ups before you're fully settled
The Costs People Forget

Hidden Costs to Plan For

These are the expenses that catch GTA movers off guard most often - budget for them up front rather than discovering them mid-move.

Deposits & Damage Costs

Both ends of the move can carry deposit-related expenses.

  • Last month's rent deposit at a new rental unit
  • Move-out cleaning fees required by some condo boards
  • Elevator booking deposits, common in Toronto condo buildings
  • Potential charges for wall or floor damage during move-out inspection

Parking & Access Fees

Dense GTA neighbourhoods often mean extra logistics costs.

  • Temporary No Parking permits for the moving truck (required by several cities)
  • Visitor parking fees at condo or apartment buildings
  • Elevator or loading dock booking fees
  • Extra time charges if street parking isn't available near the entrance

Insurance & Protection

Worth budgeting for even if you never need to use it.

  • Basic carrier liability coverage is often limited - full-value protection costs more
  • Extra coverage for high-value items (art, electronics, antiques)
  • Tenant or homeowner insurance updates for the new address
  • Valuation coverage for a long-distance move, which carries more risk than local
Keeping Costs Down

Money-Saving Tips That Actually Work

A few decisions early in the process make a bigger dent in your final bill than most people expect.

Timing Your Move

When you move affects what you pay.

  • Mid-month and mid-week moves are typically cheaper than weekends or month-end
  • Avoid the May-to-September peak season if your dates are flexible
  • Book several weeks ahead - last-minute bookings often cost more

Do What You Can Yourself

Full-service isn't all-or-nothing.

  • Pack non-fragile items yourself and let the crew handle only furniture and fragiles
  • Source free boxes from local stores or community groups instead of buying new
  • Handle small-item transport (a few carloads) yourself to shrink the truck load

Get an Accurate Quote Early

Surprises on invoice day are the most common budget-buster.

  • Get a fixed, written quote rather than a rough phone estimate
  • Confirm what's included: stairs, long carries, disassembly, appliances
  • Ask about any conditions that would change the price on the day
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I budget for a local GTA move?

It depends heavily on home size, distance, and how much packing help you need - a fixed, written quote from us after a quick look at your move details is more reliable than a general estimate.

What's the biggest cost people forget to budget for?

Condo elevator booking fees and building deposits catch a lot of GTA movers off guard, along with utility connection fees at the new address - both are easy to miss if you're only thinking about the moving company's bill.

Does booking further in advance actually save money?

Yes, generally. Last-minute bookings, especially during the May-to-September peak season, tend to cost more and offer less date flexibility than moves booked a few weeks ahead.
